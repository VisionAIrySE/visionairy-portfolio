## The core argument

Stop asking "how do we contact 10,000 businesses" and start asking "which 500
have something happening **right now** that makes this relevant." Event-triggered
outbound rather than cold outbound.

And a sharper definition of personalisation:

> "Hey John, I saw you're the owner of ABC Construction" is not personalisation.
> That is mail merge. Real personalisation is: *I found something about your
> business that makes me believe I can make you money or save you money.*

## Signals worth detecting

Hiring salespeople · hiring administrative staff · new location · new owner ·
website recently changed · poor Google reviews · no chat or AI capability ·
slow response to enquiries · high-value leads going unanswered · running paid
advertising · recently funded · new product or service · recruiting heavily ·
poor SEO · a competitor gaining traction · owner posting about growth or
problems · technology that looks dated · job descriptions that describe an
expensive manual process.

## The proposed cadence

- **Day 0 — research.** Build a dossier: company, owner, industry, website,
  LinkedIn, email, phone, growth signals, pain signals, technology, likely pain,
  potential ROI, personalisation hook. Score it.
- **Day 1 — LinkedIn.** No pitch. Connect or interact on something genuinely
  relevant, so they can look you up. LinkedIn is the credibility layer.
- **Day 2 — email.** Short and specific. *"I noticed you're hiring three more
  salespeople. That usually creates a fairly predictable problem…"* Then name
  the problem, then: *"I built something that addresses exactly that. Worth
  showing you what I mean?"* No brochure, no 800 words, no calendar link yet.
- **Day 4 — LinkedIn again, with a personalised micro-video.** 60–120 seconds,
  screen recording of THEIR business. *"Hey John — Russ Wright here. I was
  looking at ABC Construction and noticed something interesting…"* This is the
  attention multiplier, and it is the piece that says *I spent time on your
  business* rather than *I want your money*.
- **Day 5–7 — phone.** By now they are not cold: name seen, profile seen, email
  received, video watched.

## Channel order should vary by prospect

- High intent — LinkedIn → email → video → phone
- Medium intent — email → LinkedIn → content → phone
- Low intent — email → LinkedIn → content → retarget → re-engage
- Very high value — research → LinkedIn engagement → personalised video →
  email → phone → direct mail

## Other channels

**YouTube** as an authority library, not an outbound channel — industry
teardowns, "5 things costing contractors leads", "I analysed 100 SMB websites",
case studies, before-and-afters. Outbound then sends the right video to the
right prospect. **Instagram** behind LinkedIn; useful for retargeting and
familiarity in consumer-facing trades, not as an acquisition engine.

## The architecture proposed

ICP engine → prospect discovery → company intelligence → trigger/signal
detection → pain and opportunity analysis → lead scoring → message strategy →
channel selection → personalised content → outreach → response classification →
meeting → CRM → learning and optimisation.

The learning loop is the point: which signal → which message → which channel →
which prospect → which outcome.

Supporting figure quoted: a LinkedIn study found 57% of buyers preferred email
for first contact, while 78% said the introduction should be personalised.

## Where this already agrees with what is built

Worth knowing before treating any of it as a rewrite:

- **Signal-based openings already exist.** A live job posting, a fax number and
  a printable form are three of the signals on that list, and where one is
  genuinely found the message opens on it.
- **"Not mail merge" is already the standard.** As of 2026-08-26 every message
  that can carry one opens with a clause read off that business's own published
  words — *"You design and build custom homes out of Redmond, so…"* — not with
  their name inserted into a template.
- **Channel order by prospect** is partially there: phone is already the primary
  channel and email exists to warm the line for the call.
- **The scoring engine** already ranks businesses on manual-work signals.

## Where it is genuinely new

1. **Signals nobody currently looks for** — new location, new owner, recent
   funding, paid advertising, review quality, site recently changed, competitor
   movement, owner posting about growth. Most need a source we do not have.
2. **Personalised micro-video.** Nothing like it exists. Probably the single
   biggest differentiator on the list and the most work.
3. **A sequence that moves across channels on a clock**, rather than three email
   touches with LinkedIn sent by hand.
4. **The learning loop** — recording which signal and which message produced
   which outcome, and letting that reorder the next run.

## The honest caution

Two of the "signals" on that list — poor Google reviews, competitor traction —
would need Google data, and there is a standing order against Google Places
requests. Anything built here has to source those elsewhere or drop them.

And the whole thing is worth far more once real replies exist. The first 30
emails will say more about which signals matter than any amount of design
tonight.
