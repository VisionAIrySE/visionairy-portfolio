# Six openings, rewritten to end on the money

Same first sentence as now, read off their own website. What changes is that
each one now finishes on what the work costs them, instead of trailing off into
"most have three or four more".

Every number below says where it came from. Where their own site gives a price,
that is the number used. Where it does not, the trade's published rate is used
and the letter says so in plain words.

---

## 1. Century Dentistry — dental

**Now:**
> People call to enroll in your membership plans, and it's the same few
> questions every time. Plenty of practices have that one covered by now, and
> still have three or four repetitive jobs costing them the same hours.

**Rewritten:**
> People call to enroll in your membership plans, and it's the same few
> questions every time. The ones who call after hours, or while the front desk
> is with a patient, mostly do not call back. A membership plan runs about $350
> a year and members stay three years, so five missed enrollments a month is
> around $60,000 of recurring revenue that never starts.

*Where the number comes from:* $350/year and three-year retention are the
published averages for in-house dental membership plans. If their site lists
their own plan price, that price is used instead.

---

## 2. Market of Choice — retail and food

**Now:**
> Catering orders and product questions come in by phone all day, and
> somebody's got to drop what they're doing to answer and route them right.
> Some places will have that one solved already.

**Rewritten:**
> Catering orders and product questions come in by phone all day, and somebody
> has to drop what they are doing to answer and route them right. The cost is
> not the minute on the phone. It is the customer standing at the counter while
> that call is taken, and the catering enquiry that reaches voicemail on a
> Friday and orders somewhere else. One $500 order a week going elsewhere is
> $26,000 a year.

*Where the number comes from:* $500 is a stated example, not a claim about
their orders. The letter says so.

---

## 3. LivWell Property Management — real estate

**Now:**
> When someone calls about your available units, you're handling the showings
> and screening them. It's the same questions every time.

**Rewritten:**
> When someone calls about your available units, you are handling the showings
> and the screening, and it is the same questions every time. Every day that
> takes is a day the unit is empty. On a $1,800 unit, one extra week of vacancy
> is $415 the owner does not get and you do not take a fee on. Ten units a year
> turning a week slower is over $4,000, and it is the owners who notice.

*Where the number comes from:* $1,800 is the Bend median rent. The daily figure
is that divided out. Their fee is whatever they charge; the letter does not
guess at it.

---

## 4. Bend Animal Emergency and Specialty Center — veterinary

**Now:**
> You're managing the appointment schedule for your specialty cases, the
> cardiology, the radioactive iodine therapy, handling reschedules and
> cancellations, the back-and-forth.

**Rewritten:**
> You are managing the schedule for the specialty cases, the cardiology, the
> radioactive iodine therapy, and every reschedule and cancellation is a
> back-and-forth. A slot that empties on Tuesday and does not get refilled is
> gone for good. A cardiology consult is several hundred dollars, and a
> specialist's day only holds so many. Two unfilled slots a week is most of a
> working day a month with nobody in the room.

*Where the number comes from:* consult pricing is not on their site, so no
figure is asserted. The cost is stated as the empty slot itself, which is not a
guess.

---

## 5. Central Oregon Precision — manufacturing

**Now:**
> Custom machine quotes come in pretty regular, someone takes the specs, runs
> the numbers, gets confirmation back. Materials tracking is the other steady
> one.

**Rewritten:**
> Custom quotes come in steadily, and someone takes the specs, runs the
> numbers, and chases the confirmation back. In machining the quote that goes
> out first usually wins. If yours takes three days and the shop down the road
> takes one, you lose jobs you were the better fit for and never hear why. One
> job a month at a few thousand dollars is a five-figure year.

*Where the number comes from:* nothing asserted about their pricing. The claim
is about response time deciding the award, which is how quoted work is bought.

---

## 6. A Naturopathy Clinic, Bend — medical

**Now:**
> You're looking through patient records before each consultation to get back
> up to speed, and that same work happens every single time you see somebody.

**Rewritten:**
> You are reading back through the record before each consultation to get up to
> speed, and that happens every single time you see somebody. At ten minutes a
> patient and twenty patients a day, that is over three hours, every day, on
> catching up rather than treating. Three hours is four more appointments you
> could have seen. At a typical naturopathic consult fee, that is a five-figure
> month in visits that never got booked.

*Where the number comes from:* ten minutes and twenty patients are stated as
the arithmetic, not as a claim about this clinic. If the site states their fee,
the letter names the dollar figure instead of "five-figure".

---

## What changed, in one line

The old ending said they are busy. Busy is not a problem to a business owner.

The new ending says what the busy costs: the customer who went elsewhere, the
slot that stayed empty, the enrollment that never started, the quote that lost.
Every one of them is money leaving, and every one of them stops when the
repetitive part is handled.
