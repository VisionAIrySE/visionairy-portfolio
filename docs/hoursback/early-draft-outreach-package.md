**Central Oregon automation audit → implementation → custom build**

## 1. The offer

**Name options:** *Five Hours Back* · *The Five Hour Audit* · *The Time Audit*
(Recommend **Five Hours Back** — it's the promise, not the process.)

**The pitch, one sentence:**
> I spend a week inside your operation and find at least five hours a week your team is losing to work a machine should be doing. If I can't find five hours, you don't pay.

**Price:** $999. Half up front, half on delivery.
**Guarantee:** find fewer than 5 hrs/week → full refund, no argument, they keep the report.
**Credit:** the full $999 comes off their implementation invoice.

**Their math:** 5 hrs/wk × 52 weeks × $100/hr of owner or senior-staff time = **$26,000 a year**.
Against $999 that's a 26-to-1 return in year one — and it repeats every year after.

**Your risk:** near zero. Any business with five or more people has someone retyping data between two systems.

---

## 2. What they actually get (the deliverable)

A short document — 6 to 10 pages, not 40 — delivered in person or on a call. Never emailed and left to sit.

**Structure — and this structure is the whole business model:**

**Part 1 — Where the time is going**
Named tasks, named people, hours per week, annualized dollars. Observed, not guessed.

**Part 2 — Fixes available today**
Off-the-shelf tools that solve it. Named products, real prices, what each one saves.
*This is the cheap implementation work. Priced low on purpose — no budget meeting required.*

**Part 3 — What nothing on the market fixes**
Their weird workflow. Their two systems that don't talk. Their industry quirk.
*This is the custom build, and it's in their hands on day one instead of you hoping to discover it in month three.*

**Part 4 — The order to do it in, and what each step costs**
Implementation quote lives **inside** this document. You present it in the delivery meeting and ask for the yes there.

## 3. The ladder (where the money actually is)

| Step | Price | Time | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Audit | $999 | 1 week | Pays for itself, gets you inside, credited forward |
| Implementation | $1,500–$3,000 | 1 week, fixed scope | No budget conversation. Builds trust. Reveals the real problem. |
| Custom build | $10,000–$40,000 | 4–10 weeks | **The goal.** Only you can do it — it's in Part 3. |
| Retainer / support | $500–$2,000/mo | ongoing | Recurring revenue once the custom thing is live |

**The trap to avoid:** drowning in $1,500 wiring jobs and never reaching custom work.
Guard: every implementation is **fixed scope, one week, named tools, written down**. No open-ended "while you're in there."

**Why the market's on your side here:** Central Oregon has no bench of AI implementers. They won't take your report and shop it, because there's nobody to shop it to.

## 4. Cold email sequence

Voice notes applied: warm open, one concrete thing to look at before any explanation, benefit not mechanism, every product named gets a link, warm close.

**The price goes in email one.** The voice profile's "no price in a first approach" note came from a complex service with no fixed number. Here the guarantee *is* the offer, and a guarantee without a number attached says nothing.

## 5. Phone script (this is the one that pays this week)

**Open — 10 seconds, no pitch:**
> "Hi [Name], this is Russ Wright, I'm local here in Bend. I'll be quick — do you have a minute?"

**Frame:**
> "I work with small operations around Central Oregon finding the hours their team loses to work a machine should be doing. Double entry, chasing paperwork, rebuilding the same report every week. That kind of thing."

**The hook — one question, then shut up:**
> "Can I ask you one thing — what's the task at your place where somebody's basically retyping information that already exists somewhere else?"

*(They will have an answer. Every one of them does. Let them talk.)*

**The offer:**
> "Here's what I do. I spend a week inside your operation and I find at least five hours a week you're losing to that stuff, with real numbers attached. It's nine ninety-nine, half up front — and if I can't find five hours, you pay nothing and you keep the report. Most places, five hours a week is about twenty-six thousand dollars a year of somebody's time."

**The close:**
> "Worth a coffee to talk about it? I can come to you."

**30 dials a day. Same script. The offer does the work.**

## 6. Objection answers

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "We're too small for that" | "Smaller usually means it's *you* doing the admin, and your hour is the expensive one." |
| "I don't have time for an audit" | "It's about ninety minutes of your time total. The rest is me watching and asking your staff questions." |
| "We already use ChatGPT" | "Good — that's a tool. What I'm looking at is where it lives in your process. Most places have the tool and still do the work by hand." |
| "$999 is a lot" | "It comes off the implementation, so if we do anything together it costs you nothing. And if I find less than five hours you get it all back." |
| "How do I know you'll find anything?" | "You don't have to. That's what the guarantee is for." |
| "Can't I do this myself?" | "You can. It'd take you forty hours and you'd be guessing at the tools. That's the part I already know." |
| "Send me some information" | "I'll send you the list for [their industry] — takes two minutes to read. Can I follow up Thursday?" |
| "We're not ready for AI" | "Neither are most of the fixes. Half of what I find is just two systems that should be talking to each other." |

## 8. Where the target list comes from

**Verified:** Deschutes County has **1,055** professional/scientific/technical services establishments alone (Census County Business Patterns, 2022). Total employer establishments across all industries is very likely in the **7,000–8,000** range for Deschutes — that figure is an estimate, not confirmed.

**Working target:** roughly **2,000–4,000** Central Oregon businesses with enough staff to have the problem and enough money to pay for the fix.

**Sources to pull from, in order of usefulness:**
1. **Oregon Secretary of State business registry** — public, free, searchable, includes registered agent addresses
2. **Bend Chamber of Commerce directory** — pre-filtered to businesses that spend money on growth
3. **Deschutes County business licenses** — public record
4. **Google Maps / Places** — best for category + review-count + phone, thin on email
5. **Redmond, Sisters, Prineville, Madras chambers** — smaller, less picked over

**Still unconfirmed:** which of these gives usable email addresses at volume. That's the one piece I need to go check.
