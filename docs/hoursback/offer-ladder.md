# The offer ladder the first message has to feed

Source: Corey Gamin's YouTube playbook on AI services, pasted by Russ in full on
2026-08-27. Russ: "I got this idea from Corey Gamin... I believe I skipped his
first step which is the free AI eval."

He is not skipping it any more. He is making it his, because targeted Discovery
is the thing Xpansion does better than anyone selling the same hour.

## Rung 1 — the free fifteen minutes

Fifteen minutes, free, to find where the repetitive work actually sits. Then
Russ goes away, researches, and comes back with a prescription:

  - one named tool
  - what it costs
  - why that one
  - the first step to putting it in

**That prescription IS the deliverable.** Nothing is built at this stage and
nothing is sold. The prospect gets something they can act on alone.

## Rung 2 — the question that opens the door

"You've got your recommendation. Do you want to implement that yourself, or do
you want my help with it?"

Gamin's number: more than half want help, or ask what else is out there. He
calls the free fifteen a tripwire — its job is to make the real product wanted.

## Rung 3 — the paid assessment, $999

Forty-five minutes instead of fifteen. Three to seven problems instead of one.
Three to seven fixes instead of one. Questions specific to their industry, which
fifteen minutes has no room for.

## What this means for the first email — READ THIS BEFORE WRITING COPY

The fifteen-minute sentence is not an invitation to a chat. It promises a
DELIVERABLE in return for their time, and it hints there is usually more than
one thing to find.

**Automation and build are not a door and a backup door.** Russ, 2026-08-30:
"Automation does not preclude Build, or vice versa." Both are live from the
first minute. Some work already has something good sold for it; some never
will. The fifteen minutes is what tells them which is which — and that
telling-which-is-which is the Xpansion edge.

**Two drafts were rejected for getting this wrong.** The first made the build
the whole offer and deleted the tool hunt entirely. The second made building a
fallback for when nothing fits. Both are wrong. The sentence has to carry the
tool hunt AND the build possibility AND a named deliverable, and only the part
about what Discovery would go looking for changes by business.
